## Research

My research focuses on combining energy networks with novel 'smart' technologies and processes, aiming to maximise utilization of each of those to increase system efficiency.

* Increasing utilization of an energy network through smart technologies means finding new ways of operating smart technologies to increase network capacity. Increasing network capacity is required to minimize requirements for costly, disruptive and unpopular network reniforcements.
* Increased utilization of smart technologies within an energy networks means finding new ways of designing, co-ordinating or connecting devices to maximise their usefulness within the system. Increased utilization can increase efficiency of the grid, reduces renewables curtailment, therefore ultimately reducing operational costs of the system.

In my RAEng Research Fellowship, I am exploring this in the context of novel hybrid systems, i.e., systems for which there is both a discrete (on-off) and continuous (smooth) characteristic. These characteristics arise when combining traditional power system technologies (e.g., network reconfiguration; protection; bang-bang/hysteresis control) with 'smart' power system technologies (e.g., network operator owned power electronics; low carbon technologies such as solar PV and electric vehicles). Combining these in novel ways can result in interesting and unexpected system behaviour and result in much more efficient utilization of both within the energy system.

**Power electronic devices for network operators**

Network operator-owned power electronic devices (PEDs) have been used in transmission networks for several decades, but are yet to be exploited by distribution network operators. I am interested in combining network reconfiguration and power electronics to enable more flexible operation that can maximise the use of utilization of both.

**Benchmarking, Tools and Data**

Power distribution systems are transitioning from largely passive, 'fit-and-forget' planning and operation strategies to active, responsive distribution systems. The requirements and scale of the models, data and tools required used for those activities is therefore undergoing a transformation.
